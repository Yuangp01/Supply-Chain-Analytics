<h2>🎯 Business Context</h2>

<p>
Modern supply chains generate large volumes of transactional and
operational data, yet businesses often struggle to connect commercial
performance with fulfillment reliability.
</p>

<p>
Revenue growth alone does not guarantee operational efficiency.
Delayed shipments, inconsistent delivery performance, and
unprofitable orders can create significant business challenges
that remain hidden in high-level sales reports.
</p>

<p>
<strong>This project demonstrates an interactive Power BI solution
that connects sales performance, shipment reliability, and product
profitability to support data-driven operational decisions.</strong>
</p>

<h2>🔍 Business Questions</h2>

<p>
The dashboard was developed to address five analytical questions:
</p>

<ol>
  <li>
    <strong>Revenue & Profitability:</strong>
    Which product categories generate the strongest financial results?
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Delivery Reliability:</strong>
    Where are shipment delays most concentrated?
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Shipping Efficiency:</strong>
    How do shipping methods compare against scheduled delivery commitments?
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Operational Risk:</strong>
    Which regions, products, and orders require further investigation?
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Financial Performance:</strong>
    Where are loss-making transactions concentrated?
  </li>
</ol>

<hr>

<h2>⚙️ Analytical Approach</h2>

<h3>01 — Data Preparation</h3>

<p>
Power Query was used to prepare transactional and operational
data for analysis, with emphasis on data consistency,
appropriate field types, and reporting readiness.
</p>

<h3>02 — Data Modeling</h3>

<p>
The Power BI data model connects order information with
customer, product, category, and date attributes to support
cross-functional reporting.
</p>

<h3>03 — DAX & KPI Development</h3>

<p>
DAX-based measures support sales, profit, order volume,
shipping duration, and delivery performance analysis
across report filter selections.
</p>

<h3>04 — Interactive Reporting</h3>

<p>
The dashboard incorporates slicers, dynamic KPI cards,
time-series charts, category comparisons, and
order-level investigation tables.
</p>

<h3>05 — Business Interpretation</h3>

<p>
Operational and financial metrics are examined together
to identify potential service issues and prioritize
follow-up analysis.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>💡 Key Analytical Findings</h2>

<h3>01 — Shipping Reliability Requires Attention</h3>

<p>
The dashboard reports a <strong>57.2% late delivery rate</strong>,
indicating a substantial concentration of shipments
that did not meet their recorded delivery classification.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Business Implication:</strong>
Delivery reliability should be investigated across
shipping methods, markets, and individual orders
before operational changes are prioritized.
</p>

<h3>02 — Positive Aggregate Profit Masks Individual Losses</h3>

<p>
Although the dashboard reports approximately
<strong>$248.63K in total profit</strong>,
it also identifies <strong>3,319 orders with losses</strong>
and approximately <strong>$214K in recorded losses</strong>
across those transactions.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Business Implication:</strong>
Category and order-level profitability analysis is
necessary to identify potential pricing, discounting,
or operational issues.
</p>

<h3>03 — Delivery Performance Varies Across Regions</h3>

<p>
The regional shipping chart shows different volumes
of late shipments across geographical markets,
with Central America and Western Europe among the
regions displaying substantial late-shipment counts.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Business Implication:</strong>
Operations teams should compare regional late rates
alongside total shipment volumes before selecting
markets for intervention.
</p>

<h3>04 — Shipping Methods Exhibit Different Service Patterns</h3>

<p>
The dashboard highlights variation in late-shipment rates
and scheduled shipping durations across Standard Class,
Second Class, First Class, and Same Day shipping.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Business Implication:</strong>
Shipping service commitments should be evaluated using
both actual and scheduled performance, rather than
comparing delivery speed alone.
</p>
